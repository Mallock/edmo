# Design Context

### Users

Elite Dangerous commanders, mid-flight. The HUD is an always-on-top overlay pinned beside a
cockpit at 420 px wide while the user is flying, mining, or on foot — read in glances, never
studied. The marketing site is read once, cold, by someone deciding whether to trust an
unsigned hobby binary with their game folder.

Two very different reading contexts, one product voice.

### Brand Personality

Instrument, not app. The operator is crew: terse, grounded, never cute. Every number on the
HUD came out of the player journal and is answerable for. The site's job is credibility —
the product's entire pitch is *nothing leaves your machine*, so the page must itself be
above suspicion.

Three words: **mechanical, grounded, unhurried**.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme: dark, non-negotiable.** Both surfaces. The HUD floats over a black cockpit at
night; a light panel would be a flashbang. The site is the same world.

**The cockpit palette is canon and fixed** (confirmed 2026-08-15):

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `--amber` | `#f0a030` | the standing job — money, timers, the operator's own voice |
| `--cyan` | `#40d0f0` | destinations and the commander's own words |
| `--green` | `#40e060` | confirmed / delivered / safe |
| `--red` | `#e03030` | expiry, failure, hull |
| `--bg` | `#0a1628` | navy, never black |

Colour is signal, not decoration. A hue means one thing across both surfaces.

**Anti-references:** generic dark-mode SaaS dashboard; neon-on-black gaming skin; anything
with a purple-to-blue gradient.

### Design Principles

1. **Density is a feature, not a failure.** 420 px and a commander who is also flying.
   Compactness is the brief — but compact is not the same as eighteen arbitrary font sizes.
   Tighten the system, keep the density.
2. **Colour carries meaning or it goes.** Amber is not "the brand colour to sprinkle."
   If a surface is amber it is because the standing job lives there.
3. **The page must practise what it sells.** A site claiming no telemetry may not phone a
   third party for a font. Credibility is a design constraint here, not just a copy claim.
4. **Emoji are the icon set.** Deliberate, and load-bearing on Linux where the emoji font
   fallback chain is the only thing keeping the buttons visible. Not a tell.
5. **Nothing snaps and nothing flashes without a reason.** Motion is state change only,
   and every animation is gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
